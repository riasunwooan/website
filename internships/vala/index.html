Intern, VALA: Veterans Affairs Claims Software

Oct 2024 – Oct 2025 · AI-Powered VA Claims Software for Attorneys, Agents & VSOs

Mentor Kyle Aquino, CEO of VALA, former U.S. Naval Officer

About VALA

VALA builds AI-powered VA claims software for the people who file disability claims on veterans’ behalf: accredited attorneys, claims agents and Veterans Service Organizations (VSOs). I worked under Kyle Aquino, VALA’s CEO and a former U.S. Naval Officer, on the social impact side of the company: finding funding, making the case for why the work matters, helping with the customer newsletter, and testing the product.

What I did

Social impact analysis

Pulled together national data on veterans, VA claims, denials and errors, and used it to find the gap VALA is built to close: about 1.2 million veterans and $19.3 billion a year in compensation that never reaches them (below).

Social impact grants list

Researched and built VALA’s list of social impact grants: foundations and programs that fund veteran wellbeing, mental health and access to benefits, organized so the team could see which ones fit VALA and when to apply.

Bob Woodruff Foundation 2025

Submitted VALA’s application to the Bob Woodruff Foundation, which funds programs that help post-9/11 veterans, service members and their families.

Customer newsletter

Helped put together VALA’s newsletter for the attorneys, agents and VSOs who use the software.

Software testing

Tested VALA’s claims software and reported bugs and confusing steps back to the team.

The gap I found

Enlistment is more than signing a contract—it is a pledge of body, mind, and future to the defense of the nation. In return, the nation makes a solemn promise: that if a veteran’s health is harmed in service, they will be cared for. Yet today, that promise is faltering. Hidden within the system are vast, unseen gaps: 1.2 million veterans who should qualify for benefits are left without support, representing nearly $19.3 billion in lost compensation every year.

This is not simply a bureaucratic failure. It is a human crisis. Veterans make up less than 7% of the U.S. adult population but account for 14% of suicide deaths. Research shows that receiving disability compensation improves financial stability and mental health, turning each approved claim into a protective factor against despair. But with 25–30% of initial claims denied and up to 18% of decisions marred by errors while applying for the claim, far too many veterans are failed by the very system built to protect them.

VALA aims to close this gap. By providing accessible, accredited legal support, VALA ensures veterans win the benefits they have earned, transforming denials into approvals, errors into justice, and missed billions into direct income, healthcare, and dignity for those who once put everything on the line for their country.

How the VA claims system looks today

Who could be filing

  • About 18–19 million veterans live in the U.S., roughly 6–7% of adults. Among working-age adults (18–64), 20.1% reported a disability in 2022, and about one in three veterans reports a disability. (Annual Disability Statistics Compendium 2025 ↗)

How much the VA handles

  • In 2024 the VA completed more than 2.5 million claims and paid more than $173 billion in benefits. (VA detailed claims data ↗)
  • At the time of my analysis, about 634,000 claims were pending, and about 134,000 were backlogged, meaning waiting more than 125 days for a decision. Every one of those is a veteran waiting on money for rent, treatment or family.

Where claims go wrong

  • Errors. The VA grades accuracy two ways. At the issue level (each separate medical condition in a claim) it is right 93–94% of the time. But a claim usually bundles several conditions, so at the claim level accuracy drops to 82–84%: 16–18% of claims, about 0.5 million a year, contain at least one mistake. (VA detailed claims data ↗)
  • Denials. Around 25–30% of initial claims are denied on the first decision, about 0.7 million a year. Common reasons include missing medical evidence, no clear link between the condition and service (the “nexus”), and missed deadlines or paperwork. (Tabak Attorneys ↗)
  • Under-filing. Some veterans never file at all, or file for fewer conditions than they have, because the process is long, confusing or discouraging.

This is a rough, first-order estimate: some denied claims are later won on appeal, and some error claims and denials overlap, so the true number could be lower; under-filing isn’t counted at all, so it could also be higher. The point is the scale: errors, denials and under-filing add up to billions of dollars that were meant for veterans.

Why it matters

Mental health

Veterans are under 7% of U.S. adults but about 14% of suicide deaths, a burden tied to trauma exposure, psychiatric disorders and barriers to care. A denied or delayed claim is one more barrier. VALA’s goal is to lower it.

Income stability

Among working-age men studied by the CBO, those receiving VA disability compensation had about 10% higher personal income than non-recipients. Their earnings from work were lower, but the payments more than made up for it. (NY Compensation Law ↗)

Each approval is protective

Every approved claim stabilizes a family’s income, opens access to VA healthcare, and changes a known risk environment for suicide. That’s why I framed VALA’s impact claim by claim.

This connects to my research on social determinants of health and mental health in U.S. veterans, which found that unemployment and other social risks predict new depression, PTSD and anxiety, and that greater overall social risk predicts suicidal ideation.

Future Work Suggestions

  • Grant pipeline: turn the grants list into a tracker with deadlines, fit scores and owners, and reuse the impact analysis as a standard section in every application.
  • Measure VALA’s own impact: track approval rates, time to decision and back pay won for veterans who used VALA, against the national averages above, so future grant applications can show results, not just need.
  • Denial-reason checklist: turn the most common denial reasons (missing evidence, no service connection, missed deadlines) into checks the software flags before a claim is submitted.
  • Mental health at the point of filing: since filing a claim can be stressful, add Veterans Crisis Line information and mental health resources inside the app, informed by my SDOH research.
  • User testing with veterans and VSOs: go beyond bug testing to sit with attorneys, agents and VSO staff, and the veterans they serve, to find where the process is confusing.
  • Plain-language guides: short, readable explainers on how claims, ratings and appeals work, for veterans who never file because the system feels impossible.
