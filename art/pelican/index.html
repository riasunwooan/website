Pelican Pocket

Community service · Designer

There are emergency kits everywhere for physical injuries, but what about our mental well-being? That question became Pelican Pocket: an illustrated origami toolkit you fold yourself, with six cards tucked inside (asking for help, breathing, resetting, progress, coping with failure, and sleep) that offer grounding strategies, reframing prompts, and reminders of the support around you.

I developed it through my Independent Study Program in visual arts, working with Jess Matzkin, our director of well-being, and visual arts teacher Melanie Carr, and shaped it with student leaders, including prefects, RAs, peer mentors and ISAs. It is now part of Loomis Chaffee’s Seminar in the Common Good, where every sophomore folds their own pocket and learns the tools inside, and it is used in Connecticut libraries.

Like our school motto, Ne cede malis (do not yield to adversity), I hope it helps people navigate hard moments, and that it keeps going long after I graduate.
